Young AI Leaders Austria took part in an SL2 and Odyssean Institute Horizon Summit workshop analysing the future of AI regulation and governance in India through the Horizon Summit methodology.

## What happened

Horizon 1 mapped stakeholders: “context setters” (investors, agencies) with high power but low direct interest; “players” (big tech, consultancies, platforms) with high power and interest; “subjects” (entry-level workers, small IP owners, young creatives) with high interest but low power; and “bystanders” who still feel indirect effects.

Horizon 2 used Causal Layered Analysis to surface scenarios such as “Technocratic Illusion,” “Big Society,” and a preferred future—“Harmony/Prosperity for All”—with balanced regulation and digital security. XLRM framed variables and levers under uncertainty. Horizon 3 stress-tested that preferred future with the Absorb–Adapt–Transform resilience framework.

## Why it matters

Governance design needs structured foresight, not only legal text. Methods like Horizon Summit help youth communities practise the systems thinking required for responsible AI policy across regions.
